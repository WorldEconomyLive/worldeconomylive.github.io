    11Method

    The score

    Up to four pillars per economy: output (factory output growth, or GDP growth, blended with electricity demand), jobs (12-month change in unemployment, inverted), prices (distance of inflation from 2%, inverted) and trade (port cargo vs a year ago). Each is compared with that country's own last 10 years using the median and median absolute deviation, leaving out the pandemic distortion (March 2020 to December 2021). An economy needs at least two pillars to be scored. Countries without monthly data are scored the same way from IMF World Economic Outlook estimates for the current year (growth, inflation, change in unemployment) against their own past 15 years, plus port cargo and electricity where available; their map tooltip says so.

    The world score is 80% the GDP-weighted average of economy scores and 20% global stress: oil and gas price shocks against their own 15-year history, and world port cargo vs a year ago.

    60+ STRONG · 45–60 STEADY · 35–45 SOFT · <35 STRESSED

    How changes are shown

    The Change over toggle at the top sets one comparison period for every panel: 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, 3Y, 5Y, 10Y, 15Y or 20Y (default 1Y). Each value is shown with its change over that period.

    Scores: points. Interest rates and bond yields (and spreads between them): basis points. Series already in percent (inflation, unemployment, growth, shares of GDP): percentage points. Levels (prices, exchange rates, trade values, cargo, electricity, freight): percent change; for 3Y and longer this is the compound annual rate, labelled "per year". Trade balances can switch sign, so their change is shown in dollars.

    For 5Y and longer, monthly and weekly series compare the latest 12-month average with the 12-month average the same number of years earlier. Quarterly series compare quarter with quarter, annual series year with year, and daily market series day with day.

    Seasonal series that are not seasonally adjusted (port cargo, imports vs exports by region, chokepoints, sea cargo by ship type, electricity demand, rail and air freight) keep a like-for-like window: the latest 28 days, month, 3 months or quarter vs the same window 1, 3, 5, 10, 15 or 20 years earlier. They show changes for 1Y and longer only. Annual sections (debt, deficits and balances; people and health) also start at 1Y. What changed, the map colors and the ticker do not follow the toggle; the map tooltip does.

    A change shows n/a when the series is too infrequent for the period (1M on quarterly data; 1M–6M on annual data), when the period reaches back before the series begins (the note under the panel gives the start date), or when the source has no observation at the comparison point. Nothing is estimated or filled in. The note under each panel gives the latest data period, the date the source published it (from the source's own metadata) and the source.
