    Cash prices

    Futures markets

    Positioning in the main US-traded futures, from the CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders report.     12Method

    The score

    Up to four pillars per economy: output (factory output growth, or GDP growth, blended with electricity demand), jobs (12-month change in unemployment, inverted), prices (distance of inflation from 2%, inverted) and trade (port cargo vs a year ago). Each is compared with that country's own last 10 years using the median and median absolute deviation, leaving out the pandemic distortion (March 2020 to December 2021). An economy needs at least two pillars to be scored. Countries without monthly data are scored the same way from annual figures for completed calendar years only (growth, inflation, change in unemployment) against their own past 15 years, plus port cargo and electricity where available; the current year's IMF forecast is never scored, and the latest completed year must be no more than 2 years old. The figures come from the IMF World Economic Outlook, or from the World Bank where the IMF publishes no series for that country; their map tooltip says so. The headline names the score band first (strong 60+, steady 45–60, subdued 35–45, under stress below 35) and then the pillar furthest from normal on average across scored economies, so the words always match the number.

    The world score is 80% the GDP-weighted average of economy scores and 20% global stress: oil and gas price shocks against their own 15-year history, and world port cargo vs a year ago.

    How changes are shown

    The Change over toggle at the top sets one comparison period for every panel: 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, 3Y, 5Y, 10Y, 15Y or 20Y (default 1Y). Each value is shown with its change over that period.

    Scores: points. Interest rates and bond yields (and spreads between them): basis points. Series already in percent (inflation, unemployment, growth, shares of GDP): percentage points. Levels (prices, exchange rates, trade values, cargo, electricity, freight): percent change; for 3Y and longer this is the compound annual rate, labelled "per year". Trade balances can switch sign, so their change is shown in dollars.

    For 5Y and longer, monthly and weekly series compare the latest 12-month average with the 12-month average the same number of years earlier. Quarterly series compare quarter with quarter, annual series year with year, and daily market series day with day.

    Seasonal series that are not seasonally adjusted (port cargo, imports vs exports by region, chokepoints, sea cargo by ship type, electricity demand, rail and air freight) keep a like-for-like window: the latest 28 days, month, 3 months or quarter vs the same window 1, 3, 5, 10, 15 or 20 years earlier. They show changes for 1Y and longer only. Annual sections (debt, deficits and balances; people and health) also start at 1Y. What changed and the map colors do not follow the toggle; the map tooltip and the ticker do.

    A change shows n/a when the series is too infrequent for the period (1M on quarterly data; 1M–6M on annual data), when the period reaches back before the series begins (the note under the panel gives the start date), or when the source has no observation at the comparison point. Nothing is estimated or filled in. The note under each panel gives the latest data period, when the source published it and the source. "Released" is a date from the source's own metadata for that series; "dataset last updated" is the date the source last updated the whole dataset the series belongs to (OECD, World Bank and Eurostat publish dates per dataset, not per series); "published daily" marks market data released each business day; where a source publishes no date the note says so. A range after "Latest" means the latest period differs between the rows of the panel; a table of past years shows "History: first to last · Latest: last".

    Viewing past dates

    The As of date next to the toggle shows the whole site as it stood on that date: every value, score, map color, What changed item and note is the latest available on or before it, and the toggle measures back from that date (1Y = one year before it). Nothing published after the chosen date is shown. Weekends and holidays show the latest data on or before them.

    As shown on that date: from the first day of the daily archive on, the site shows exactly what it showed that day. Every day the run keeps a copy of the site's data and of every input series as fetched (new and revised figures), a record that cannot be rebuilt later.

    Rebuilt from data as published today: for earlier dates the site is rebuilt from the sources' current history, so figures include later revisions. Each figure counts as released after its source's usual delay after the end of the period it covers: OECD inflation 40 days, unemployment and factory output 45, GDP 60, bond yields 10, merchandise trade 50; BIS policy rates 5; Eurostat inflation 20, unemployment 30, factory output and GDP 45, air freight 100, rail freight 120; World Bank Pink Sheet 5; Ember 45; US BTS freight 75; Statistics Canada 55; Monthly Treasury Statement 12; IMF PortWatch 7 (the latest 10 days are left out as today); ECB exchange rates and Treasury yields the same day; fed funds and federal debt 1. IMF World Economic Outlook figures for a year count as released with that year's October WEO (15 October), World Bank WDI figures on 1 July of the following year. Scores before the archive began are rebuilt the same way, applying today's method to each past date.

    The earliest date offered is the first date for which at least one score can be computed. Series that had not begun by the chosen date show n/a with "history begins …" in the note.

    Sources, licences and status

    Every panel's note names its source and carries the credit line that source's terms ask for. All figures are free public data, shown with credit, with the site's own calculations (changes, windows, ratios, scores) stated as such; no source endorses this site or is responsible for these calculations. Port data lags, so the latest 10 days are excluded.

    The data files the site loads (under /data, including the history behind past dates) carry the same sources and terms, listed in a TERMS.txt in each folder. Anyone reusing them must credit each source and follow its terms; commercial reuse of IMF data requires the IMF’s permission.
